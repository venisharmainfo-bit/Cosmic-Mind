02 · Space Time Gravity

Gravity Is Not
A Force

Einstein's general relativity revealed that gravity is the curvature of spacetime caused by mass. Massive objects warp the fabric itself — other objects simply follow the curves. Time runs measurably slower near large masses. In 2015, LIGO detected gravitational waves: ripples in spacetime from two black holes merging 1.3 billion light-years away.

04 · Black Holes & Singularities

Where Physics
Breaks Down

At a singularity, every known law of physics ceases to function. Black holes are regions where gravity is so extreme that not even light can escape the event horizon. Hawking radiation predicts they slowly evaporate — raising the information paradox: if quantum information is destroyed, it violates the fundamental unitarity of quantum mechanics.

The 95% We
Cannot See

Everything you have ever observed — every star, planet, photon of light — accounts for just 5% of the total energy content of the universe. The remaining 95% is dark matter and dark energy: invisible, directly undetected, yet deduced from gravitational fingerprints. Dark energy is driving the accelerating expansion of the cosmos.

07 · Cosmic Theory

The Origin and Fate
of Everything

The Big Bang was not an explosion inside space — it was an expansion of space itself, 13.8 billion years ago. Inflation proposes an epoch of exponential expansion in the first 10⁻³² seconds, smoothing the early universe. The cosmos will end in Heat Death: maximum entropy, infinite cold, total equilibrium. No gradients. No stars. No life.

08 · Consciousness

The Hard Problem
of Experience

Why does physical processing in the brain give rise to subjective experience? Why is there something it is like to be you? We can map every neuron, trace every synapse — and still not explain the redness of red, the ache of nostalgia, the taste of salt. No complete answer exists. This remains the deepest open question at the edge of science and philosophy.
